About Press Corps

A specialized media committee at SEAMUN I. You represent a news outlet, cover committee debate, interview delegates, and publish under deadline — guided by journalistic ethics rather than standard GA procedure.

What you do

Observe debate across committees, investigate claims, run interviews and press conferences, and write balanced coverage that holds the conference’s “Policies with a Purpose” theme accountable.

How it differs

Press Corps delegates do not represent countries. You are assigned a news agency, follow special media RoP, and are led by editors rather than traditional chairs.

Conference context

SEAMUN I is a student-led, non-profit Model UN focused on global healthcare and sustainability. 100% of surplus is donated to the Thai Red Cross Society.

Agenda topic

One beginner topic for the full committee.

📰

The Question of Determining Journalistic Ethics in Reporting on Public Health and Global Affairs

Beginner

Delegates weigh press freedom, public interest, accuracy, and harm when covering sensitive public-health and global-affairs stories — the same ethical lines you will walk while reporting live at SEAMUN I.

FAQ

Do Press Corps delegates represent countries?

No. You are allocated a news agency and work as journalists and editorial staff.

How many topics does Press Corps have?

One: journalistic ethics in reporting on public health and global affairs (beginner).

Is prior MUN experience required?

No. Press Corps is beginner-level and open to grades 7–12 and university students.

What is an editor vs a delegate?

Editors (2) lead the committee like chairs. Delegates (15) report, interview, and publish under editorial guidance.

Where do I register?

Use the forms on seamun.com/forms for delegate, advisor, and chair/editor applications.
